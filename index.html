Antonio Ferrer-Sánchez

Physicist working on machine learning for physics

I build physics-informed neural networks: models trained against the equations a system obeys, not only against data. I use them to solve and control physical systems, from relativistic fluids and black-hole oscillations to quantum control and sensing.

I am a Quantum Algorithm Engineer at Kipu Quantum and a PhD student at the Universitat de València, in the Intelligent Data Analysis Laboratory.

Research

Three lines of work, each with a tutorial post and the papers behind it.

Scientific machine learning

Physics-informed neural networks for differential equations, including systems with sharp transitions and coupled dynamics, and transformer-based particle tracking.

  • Gradient-annihilated PINNs for solving Riemann problems: Application to relativistic hydrodynamics 2024
  • Trackformers: in search of transformer-based particle tracking for the high-luminosity LHC era 2025
Read the tutorial: Physics-informed machine learning: from physical laws to neural solvers →

Quantum dynamics and control

Neural approaches to counterdiabatic driving, which suppresses unwanted transitions during quantum evolution, and control protocols that raise the quantum Fisher information of a sensor.

  • Physics-informed neural networks for an optimal counterdiabatic quantum computation 2024
  • Physics-Informed Neural Networks for Maximizing Quantum Fisher Information in Time-Dependent Many-Body Systems 2026
Read the tutorial: From solving equations to controlling quantum systems →

Relativistic physics

Physics-informed learning for relativistic hydrodynamics, the gravitational collapse of a scalar field, and the oscillation frequencies of rotating black holes.

  • Kerr Quasinormal Modes without Variable Separation: A Two-Dimensional Hyperboloidal Teukolsky Solver with Physics-Informed Neural Networks 2026
  • Addressing the gravitational collapse of a massless scalar field with physics-informed neural networks 2026
